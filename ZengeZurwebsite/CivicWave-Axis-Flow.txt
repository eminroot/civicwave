This document explains how the CivicWave admin dashboard works, end to end.

  • PART 1 — NON-TECHNICAL VERSION  (for managers, officials, judges)
  • PART 2 — TECHNICAL VERSION      (for developers)

The dashboard is the "back office" web app used by the local government
(Nərimanov district, Baku) to receive, triage, assign, and resolve citizen
reports about city problems (potholes, garbage, broken lights, flooding, etc.).
Citizens submit these reports from a separate CivicWave mobile app; this website
is where municipal staff manage them.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. THE BIG PICTURE — WHAT THIS WEBSITE IS FOR
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

A citizen sees a problem in the street (e.g. an overflowing garbage bin),
opens the CivicWave phone app, takes a photo, and submits it.

Behind the scenes, an Artificial Intelligence (AI) looks at that photo and
automatically figures out:
  - WHAT the problem is        (category, e.g. "Cleaning & Waste")
  - HOW serious it is          (severity: Low / Medium / High / Critical)
  - WHICH department           should handle it
  - HOW urgent it is           (a priority score from 0 to 100)
  - ESTIMATED repair cost       (a rough guess in AZN / ₼)

That report then appears INSTANTLY on this website, where government staff
take over: they approve it, assign a field worker, the worker fixes it and
uploads an "after" photo, the AI double-checks the fix, and the case is closed.
The citizen gets points for a confirmed, useful report.

In short, the website turns a phone photo into an organized, trackable
municipal work order.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2. WHO USES IT (THREE ROLES)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

You can switch roles from the card at the bottom-left of the sidebar. Each role
sees a different set of menus and can do different things:

  (A) DISTRICT MANAGER — "Rayon Meneceri" (Aysel Abbasova)
      - Sees EVERYTHING across ALL departments.
      - Can approve/reject reports, reassign them to any department,
        review proof, close cases, export reports.
      - Has access to all menus including Analytics, Departments, Audit.

  (B) DEPARTMENT HEAD — "Departament Rəhbəri" (Rəşad Quliyev)
      - Sees ONLY their own department's reports (e.g. Roads).
      - Can approve, reassign within their department, review proof, close.
      - Sees a smaller menu (no district-wide Departments/Audit panels).

  (C) FIELD WORKER — "Sahə İşçisi" (Tural Əliyev)
      - Sees ONLY the tasks assigned to them.
      - Their job is to go fix the problem and upload before/after photos.
      - Simple menu: My Tasks, Map, Settings.

3. THE SCREEN LAYOUT (WHAT'S ALWAYS ON SCREEN)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  - LEFT SIDEBAR: the CivicWave logo, the navigation menu (grouped into
    "İdarəetmə / Management", "Analiz / Analysis", "Sistem / System"), and at
    the bottom the role-switcher card showing who you are logged in as.
    Some menu items show a small red number = how many items need attention.

  - TOP BAR: a label + the page title on the left, and a search box on the
    right. You can search by report ID, address, or keyword (press "/" to jump
    to it). Searching automatically takes you to the list of reports.

  - MAIN AREA: the content of whichever page you selected.

  - TOAST MESSAGES: small confirmation pop-ups appear at the bottom whenever
    you do something (e.g. "Report #7 approved").

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
4. THE PAGES — WHAT EACH ONE SHOWS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

4.1  ÜMUMİ BAXIŞ (OVERVIEW / DASHBOARD HOME)
     The "control room" summary. It shows:
       - A row of KEY NUMBERS (KPIs): open reports, awaiting approval, in
         progress, overdue, resolved this week, and average resolution time —
         each with a small trend line and a "vs last week" change.
       - SON MÜRACIƏTLƏR (Latest Reports): a live feed of the newest reports
         with photo thumbnail, severity tag, category, and address.
       - RİSK ALTINDA (At Risk): reports that are overdue or about to miss
         their deadline, with how much time is left.
       - KATEQORİYALAR ÜZRƏ (By Category): a bar breakdown of which problem
         types are most common.
       - DEPARTAMENT PERFORMANSI (only for the Manager): each department's
         workload and SLA (service-level / on-time) percentage.

4.2  CANLI XƏRİTƏ (LIVE MAP)
     An interactive map of the district. Every report is a colored pin
     (color = severity, icon = category). You can switch between "pins" and a
     "heat" view. Clicking a pin (or a row in the side list) opens that
     report's full detail. Critical problems show as bigger pins.

4.3  SORĞU NÖVBƏSİ (REQUEST QUEUE)
     The main working list — a sortable table of all reports you're allowed to
     see. Each row shows: photo, title + ID + address, severity, status,
     who it's assigned to, the AI confidence %, the priority score, and the
     deadline. You can FILTER by severity, category, status, and department,
     and SORT by any column. Overdue rows are marked with a red edge.
     Click any row to open the full report.

4.4  TAPŞIRIQLARIM (MY TASKS) — field worker only
     A card view of the jobs assigned to that worker, sorted by deadline, each
     with a countdown timer showing time remaining.

4.5  ANALİTİKA (ANALYTICS)
     A clean statistics dashboard:
       - Four summary cards: total reports, resolved, average resolution time,
         and SLA compliance (each with a +/- change badge).
       - A TREND chart (smooth line) of daily report volume over the last days.
       - A STATUS DISTRIBUTION donut chart (how many are pending / approved /
         in progress / resolved / etc.).
       - A CATEGORY breakdown and a KEY METRICS panel (avg approval time, avg
         resolution time, SLA, reopen rate).

4.6  DEPARTAMENTLƏR (DEPARTMENTS) — manager only
     One card per department showing: number of staff, total reports, how many
     are open, how many are overdue, and the team members.

4.7  HESABATLAR (REPORTS / EXPORT)
     For exporting data. Shows summary stat cards, a date-range selector
     (this week / this month / custom), and two export options — Excel (CSV)
     and PDF / Print — plus a live preview table of the rows that will be
     exported. Filters from the Queue carry over.

4.8  AUDİTOR MODULU (AUDITOR MODULE) — manager only, READ-ONLY
     A watchdog view listing "anomalies": reopened cases, rejected reports, and
     SLA violations. Nothing can be changed here — it's purely for oversight.

4.9  PARAMETRLƏR (SETTINGS)
     Shows your profile and access level, plus notification toggles (overdue
     alerts, new-assignment alerts, weekly report, proof-approval alerts).

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
5. OPENING A SINGLE REPORT (THE DETAIL PAGE)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Clicking any report (from the feed, queue, map, etc.) opens a full detail page:

  - The citizen's PHOTO of the problem.
  - The AI ANALYSIS: a short written description of what the AI detected in the
    photo, plus boxes for Category, Estimated Cost, Severity, and Confidence %.
  - VƏTƏNDAŞ QEYDİ (Citizen Note): any text the citizen added (or "no note").
  - YER (Location): the address, GPS coordinates, and a mini-map.
  - HADİSƏLƏR XRONOLOGİYASI (Timeline): step-by-step history of the case —
    submitted → AI analyzed → approved → assigned → in progress → proof
    uploaded → resolved.
  - On the right, an ACTION PANEL that changes depending on the report's status
    and your role (see the lifecycle below).

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
6. THE LIFECYCLE OF A REPORT (STEP BY STEP)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  STEP 1 — SUBMITTED (Təsdiq gözləyir / Pending)
     Citizen submits via the phone app. AI categorizes it. It lands in the
     queue awaiting a manager/head decision.

  STEP 2 — APPROVE or REJECT
     Manager/Head opens it and either:
       - APPROVES  → citizen earns points, case moves forward; or
       - REJECTS   → marked as spam/invalid; or
       - REASSIGNS → sends it to a different (correct) department.

  STEP 3 — ASSIGNED & IN PROGRESS (Davam edir)
     The report goes to a department and a field worker starts the work.

  STEP 4 — PROOF UPLOADED (Sübut gözləyir)
     The worker uploads BEFORE and AFTER photos as proof of the fix. This goes
     to the supervisor for confirmation.

  STEP 5 — AI FIX-CHECK + CLOSE (Həll edildi / Resolved)
     When closing, the manager uploads the "after" photo. The AI compares it to
     the original problem and gives a verdict: "fix successful" or "not enough"
     with a confidence %. If the AI approves (or the manager manually overrides),
     the case is CLOSED, the citizen is notified, and points are confirmed.

     A closed case can be REOPENED if the problem comes back — these show up in
     the Auditor module as anomalies.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
7. THE AI FEATURES (IN PLAIN TERMS)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  - INTAKE AI (on the mobile/citizen side): reads the submitted photo and fills
    in category, severity, department, priority score, cost estimate, and a
    written explanation. The dashboard displays these results.

  - FIX-VERIFICATION AI (on this dashboard): when a worker/manager uploads the
    "after" photo, the system sends it to Google's Gemini AI along with the
    original problem description and asks: "Is this actually fixed?" Gemini
    replies yes/no with a confidence score and a reason. This prevents closing
    cases that aren't really resolved.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
8. LIVE DATA & DEMO MODE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  - When connected to the government's database (Firebase), the dashboard shows
    REAL reports submitted from the phone app and updates automatically every
    few seconds — no need to refresh.
  - When not connected, it runs in DEMO MODE with a small sample dataset so the
    interface still works for presentations.
  - All actions (approve, reassign, close, etc.) are written back to the
    database so the phone app and dashboard stay in sync.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
A. TECH STACK & ARCHITECTURE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  - Pure front-end SPA: vanilla HTML + CSS + JavaScript. No framework, no build
    step, no bundler. Runs from static files (served here via python http.server).
  - Rendering model: a single #view container is re-rendered by template-literal
    functions on every state change (string-building, innerHTML swap). There is
    no virtual DOM; `render()` is the central re-render entry point.
  - Map: Leaflet 1.9.4 with the CARTO "light_all" basemap (loaded from CDN).
  - Fonts: Inter (Google Fonts). JetBrains Mono was removed; the legacy `--mono`
    CSS token now resolves to Inter, with `font-variant-numeric:tabular-nums`
    for figure alignment.
  - Backend (optional): Firebase Realtime Database + Firebase Storage, accessed
    via REST (no Firebase SDK / no ES modules) for robustness across file://,
    http, and CDN/cache issues.
  - AI: Google Gemini (default model "gemini-2.5-flash") via the
    generativelanguage REST endpoint, called directly from the browser.
  - Language/locale: Azerbaijani (az-Latn) UI strings throughout.

  FILE MAP
    index.html          App shell: sidebar, topbar, #view, #toasts. Loads fonts,
                        Leaflet, then the scripts (cache-busted with ?v=N).
    styles.css          Full design system: CSS custom properties (:root tokens
                        for color/space/shadow/fonts), component styles, charts,
                        responsive breakpoints.
    app.js              The application: icons, domain data (CAT/SEV/ST/ROLES),
                        demo seed, helpers, all view renderers, detail panel,
                        actions, event delegation, and the Firebase bridge
                        (window.CIVIC.*).
    gemini-config.js    { apiKey, model } for Gemini.
    gemini.js           window.CivicGemini.verifyFix(report, base64, mime).
    firebase-config.js  Firebase project config + FIREBASE_ENABLED flag.
    firebase.js         window.CIVIC_FB.* — REST polling load, patch, and
                        closeWithPhoto (Storage upload + DB write).

  SCRIPT LOAD ORDER (in index.html):
    leaflet → firebase-config → gemini-config → gemini → app → firebase
    (app.js defines window.CIVIC.* before firebase.js starts polling it.)

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
B. STATE MODEL (app.js `state`)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  state = {
    role,            // 'manager' | 'head' | 'worker'
    view,            // current view key, or 'detail'
    detailReturn,    // view to return to after closing a detail
    sel,             // currently opened report object (detail)
    proof,           // { before, after } data-URLs for worker proof upload
    fix,             // { url, file, verdict, verifying, error, manual } close flow
    filters,         // { sev, cat, status, dept, q }
    sort,            // { key, dir }
    mapLayer,        // 'pins' | 'heat'
  }

  Re-render is always via render(). A wrapper around render() also keeps the
  queue count (#qCount) in sync after each render.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
C. DOMAIN MODEL (constants in app.js)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  CAT      Categories keyed by mobile-app categoryId (roads, water, sanitation,
           greenery, lighting, facades, signage, parks, construction, seasonal,
           other). Each has: name, dept, urgency weight, icon, and a derived
           duotone palette via mkpal(hex) (mix() toward white/black).
  CATALIAS Maps legacy demo keys (road/clean/green/light/infra) → real ids.
  SEV      Severity levels: low / mid / high / crit (name + css class + weight).
  ST       Status lifecycle with a numeric `step`:
             pending(0) → approved(1) → progress(2) → proof(3) → resolved(4),
             rejected(-1).
  ST_DB    Dashboard status → value written back to the DB
             (progress/proof both map to 'in_progress').
  DEPTS    Unique department list derived from CAT.
  ROLES    Per-role: name, title, init, dept, `can` capability flags
           (approve, reassign, proof, close, export, allDept, uploadProof),
           and `nav` (allowed view keys).
  SEVHEX   Severity → hex used for map pins / mini-map.

  REPORTS  The active in-memory array of report objects. Each report (R(...))
           has: id, docId (Firebase key), title, cat, sev, status, worker,
           created, deadline, conf (AI confidence), score (priority), cost,
           lat/lng, addr, note, reporter, aiExplanation, reopened, plus
           proof/fixed-photo fields added during the lifecycle.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
D. RENDER PIPELINE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  render():
    1. add the .view-enter animation class.
    2. if state.view === 'detail': set topbar text, renderNav(), inject
       viewDetail(), initMiniMap(), reset scroll, return.
    3. else: look up NAVMETA[view] for eyebrow + title, renderNav(), then call
       the matching view function and inject its HTML:
         overview→viewOverview, map→viewMap (then initMap()), queue→viewQueue,
         tasks→viewTasks, analytics→viewAnalytics, dept→viewDept,
         reports→viewReports, audit→viewAudit, settings→viewSettings.

  renderNav():  builds the sidebar from NAVGROUPS, filtered by ROLES[role].nav;
                adds count/alert badges (pending count on queue, overdue count
                on map, open count on tasks).
  renderRole(): fills the role-switcher card and the role menu.

  Data scoping:
    scopedReports() — worker: only their assigned; head: only their dept;
                      manager: all.
    filtered()      — applies state.filters to scopedReports() then sorts by
                      state.sort (deadline/age/score/conf/field).

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
E. KEY HELPERS / CHART PRIMITIVES (all inline SVG, no chart lib)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  photo(r, which)     <img> from Firebase Storage URL, else a generated SVG
                      thumbnail (thumb()).
  ring(pct,...)       SVG donut progress ring (AI confidence indicators).
  trendline(data,hex) full-bleed KPI sparkline (area + line).
  smoothPath(pts)     Catmull-Rom-style cubic path generator.
  areaChart(data)     Analytics trend chart (gridlines + gradient area + line
                      + end dot), viewBox 600x210, non-scaling stroke.
  donut(segs,center)  Analytics status donut with center label.
  sevTag / statusTag / catIcon / glyph / initials / azn / ageStr / deadlineStr /
  fmtDate / esc       formatting + escaping helpers.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
F. EVENT HANDLING (delegated on document)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  A single click listener uses closest([data-*]) delegation:
    [data-nav]      → go(view)
    [data-open]     → openDetail(id)  (with optional [data-fly] map flyTo)
    [data-proof]    → openDetail(id)  (stops propagation)
    [data-close]    → closeDetail()
    [data-act]      → handleAction(act)
    [data-fsev]     → set severity filter
    [data-maplayer] → switch pins/heat
    [data-export]   → 'csv' → exportCSV(); else window.print()
    #roleCard       → toggle role menu
    [data-role]     → switch role (resets filters, jumps to role's first view)
    [data-sort]     → toggle/sort column

  change listener: filter selects ([data-fcat/fstatus/fdept]) and file inputs
                   ([data-file] proof slots, [data-fixfile] fix photo →
                   FileReader → data-URL → state).
  dblclick:        dropzone demo-fill with a generated SVG (presentation aid).
  input:           #globalSearch (200ms debounce) → sets q filter and routes to
                   queue/tasks.
  keydown:         "/" focuses search; "Escape" closes the detail / role menu.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
G. ACTIONS (state transitions)  — handleAction(act) on state.sel
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  approve       → setStatus 'approved'   (+ DB patch status)
  reject        → setStatus 'rejected'
  reassign      → pickDept(r): modal of departments (scoped by can.reassign:
                  'dept' vs 'any') → sets r.dept + DB patch {department}.
  submitProof   → copies state.proof.before/after → r; setStatus 'proof'.
  approveProof  → setStatus 'resolved'.
  rejectProof   → setStatus 'progress'  (sends back).
  verifyFix     → calls Gemini (see H).
  overrideFix   → state.fix.manual = true (manual approval path).
  closeCase     → see H.

  setStatus(r, st, msg, kind): mutates r.status, toasts, then writes BOTH
                  /status (numeric flag) and /statusText (lifecycle text) to the
                  DB via fbPatch, then render.
  fbPatch(r,obj): only writes if Firebase enabled AND r.docId exists.

  DB STATUS FIELDS (per record, keyed by the record name):
    /status      NUMBER  → 1 = müraciət təsdiqlənib (approved and beyond),
                           0 = pending or rejected. Consumed by the MOBILE app
                           to show "approved".  approvedFlag(st) computes it:
                           1 for approved/progress/proof/resolved, else 0.
    /statusText  STRING  → the dashboard's lifecycle value
                           (pending/approved/in_progress/proof/resolved/rejected),
                           via ST_DB. The dashboard reads this back (lifecycleOf()
                           in mapDoc) and never relies on the numeric /status.
    Record keys: the dashboard uses each /reports/<key> name as the report id and
    patches status back to that same key, so /status always maps to its
    application. Keys are assigned at CREATION (mobile app). For 1,2,3… keys the
    mobile app should allocate from a counter (e.g. /reports/counters/lastId via a
    transaction) — the dashboard already ignores a "counters" node when reading.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
H. AI FIX-VERIFICATION + CASE CLOSE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  verifyFix() (app.js → gemini.js):
    - requires state.fix.url (the uploaded "after" image as data-URL) and a
      Gemini key (window.CivicGemini.enabled).
    - extracts base64 + mime, calls CivicGemini.verifyFix(report, base64, mime).
    - gemini.js POSTs {prompt + inline_data image} to
      generativelanguage…/{model}:generateContent?key=…
      with temperature 0.1 and responseMimeType application/json.
    - prompt (in Azerbaijani) describes the original problem and asks STRICT
      JSON: {resolved:boolean, confidence:number, reason:string}.
    - parseJson() strips ``` fences and slices the {...} object.
    - returns { approved: resolved && confidence>=70, resolved, confidence,
      reason, raw }. The verdict card renders wait/ok/no/err states.

  closeCase() (app.js → firebase.js):
    - if Firebase + docId: CIVIC_FB.closeWithPhoto(docId, file|dataURL, byName,
      verdict): uploads the photo to Storage (REST POST to
      firebasestorage…/o?name=…), builds the download URL with downloadTokens,
      then PATCHes /reports/<id> with {status:'resolved', fixedImageUrl,
      fixedStoragePath, resolvedBy, resolvedAt, fixVerdict}.
    - else (demo): keeps the local data-URL as fixedImageUrl.
    - sets r.status='resolved', r.resolvedBy, r.fixVerdict; clears state.fix.

  Close is gated: the "İşi Bağla" button is enabled only when the AI verdict is
  approved OR the manager used the manual override.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
I. FIREBASE BRIDGE (REST, polling)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  Enabled when FIREBASE_ENABLED===true and cfg.databaseURL is set.

  firebase.js:
    - load(): GET {BASE}/{NODE}.json?ts=… (cache:'no-store'). Computes a
      JSON signature; if unchanged, skips re-render (cheap diff). Otherwise maps
      each child via window.CIVIC.mapDoc(key, value) → window.CIVIC.ingestLive.
      Skips the "counters" node. Polls every 4000 ms (near real-time).
    - patch(id,obj): PATCH a report node, then load().
    - closeWithPhoto(...): Storage upload + DB patch (see H).

  app.js Firebase bridge (window.CIVIC):
    - normCat()/normSev()/normStatus(): normalize free-text / multilingual DB
      values to internal keys (handles AZ + EN synonyms and numeric codes).
    - mapDoc(): converts a raw DB record into the internal report shape.
    - ingestLive(rows): replaces REPORTS, recomputes, setConn('live'|'empty'),
      re-renders. Falls back to DEMO_SEED + setConn('empty') when DB is empty.
    - setConn(kind,n): (the topbar status chip was removed from the UI, so this
      now no-ops on the missing element via an early `if(!el) return`).

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
J. EXPORT
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  exportCSV(): uses filtered() (or scopedReports() if no filter), builds a CSV
  with a UTF-8 BOM (Excel-friendly), columns: ID, Başlıq, Kateqoriya, Ciddilik,
  Status, Departament, Təyinat, Prioritet, Etibarlılıq, Xərc, Ünvan, Son tarix;
  triggers a Blob download (civicwave-hesabat.csv).
  PDF / Print option simply calls window.print() (print-to-PDF).

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
K. DESIGN SYSTEM NOTES (styles.css)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  - :root tokens: clean cool-white surfaces (--canvas #F7F8FA, --surface #FFF),
    near-black ink (--ink #14161A) and a gray ramp (ink-2..ink-4), hairline
    borders, neutral shadows, teal brand (--teal #0F5057) + vermilion accent,
    severity + status hue tokens, radius/shadow scales.
  - Typography: Inter for body/headings/data; tabular numerals globally.
  - Components: .ledger KPI strip, .card, .table queue, .stat + .chartcard +
    .donut analytics, .expopt export cards, .riskrow, slide-over/full-page
    .detail, .timeline, .toast, map pins, Firebase/AI verdict cards.
  - Responsive: breakpoints at 1280 / 1180 / 900 / 560 px; sidebar collapses
    under 900px.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
L. CAVEATS / DEMO BEHAVIOR
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  - The demo seed is small (~5 reports); Analytics trend/delta numbers are
    illustrative placeholders, not computed from the live count.
  - All API keys (Gemini, Firebase) are client-side — acceptable for a hackathon
    demo, but a production build should proxy these through a server.
  - Assets are cache-busted with ?v=N in index.html; bump N after edits and
    hard-refresh (Ctrl+F5).
